Abstract—This paper addresses multi-UA V tracking of a small
set of key targets in the presence of numerous non-key targets and
false alarms. Identity-bearing surveillance reports are intermit-
tent and inaccurate, whereas high-rate onboard measurements
are more precise but anonymous. We propose an uncertainty-
aware identity-anchored belief-propagation joint probabilistic
data association (IA-BP-JPDA) method to resolve the resulting
association ambiguity. For each key target, a covariance-aware
anchor combines the recursive prediction with the latest same-
identity surveillance report to guide association of anonymous
onboard measurements, while a bounded fallback maintains
short-term track continuity during measurement loss. IA-BP-
JPDA is training-free and requires no target-specific motion
data. Evaluation over 100 paired Monte Carlo runs shows
improved tracking accuracy and identity continuity over rep-
resentative MTT baselines, with better tracks also improving
closed-loop defender pursuit. IA-BP-JPDA reduces the time-
averaged GOSPA by51.6%over a BP-based JPDA baseline,
while ablation results support the contributions of the proposed
measurement-association and anchor-management mechanisms.
Index Terms—Multi-UA V tracking, multi-target tracking, data
association, belief propagation, heterogeneous sensing.
I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UA V) swarms provide high mo-
bility and rapid deployment for sensing and monitoring appli-
cations, while unauthorized UA Vs also pose increasing risks
to critical infrastructures. A practical counter-UA V architecture
combines wide-area surveillance with mobile defender UA Vs:
an upstream system detects and identifies targets, while de-
fender UA Vs approach selected targets for close-range sensing
and continuous tracking [1], [2]. Because defender resources
are limited, maintaining close-range tracks for every observed
UA V is generally infeasible in dense scenarios.
Motivated by threat-aware sensing and resource allocation
[3], [4], we consider an upstream surveillance system that
designates a small subset of UA Vs as key targets based
on threat assessment. It provides identity-bearing state re-
ports with relatively large uncertainty, whereas defender-UA V
onboard sensors provide more accurate and frequent but
anonymous measurements. Each onboard measurement may
therefore originate from a key target, a non-key target, or
a false alarm. This creates a data-association problem for
multi-target tracking (MTT), where the tracker must estimate
the states of multiple targets while also resolving the unknown
correspondence between measurements and targets.
Belief propagation (BP) provides an efficient approximation
to marginal target–measurement association probabilities and
has been widely used for scalable multi-target tracking [5]–[7].
These marginals can be incorporated into a joint probabilistic
data association (JPDA) update. In this work, we refer to this
BP-based JPDA implementation as BP-JPDA. However, when
a few key targets are surrounded by many kinematically simi-
lar non-key targets, competing anonymous measurements can
receive comparable association probabilities. A high-weight
non-key measurement may then pull a key-target posterior
toward a distractor, biasing the next prediction and causing
recursive identity errors [6]. In a mobile sensing loop, such
errors additionally misguide defender motion and degrade
subsequent sensing geometry.
To address this problem, we propose an uncertainty-aware
identity-anchored BP-JPDA framework, termed IA-BP-JPDA.
It combines each key track’s recursive motion prediction
with the latest available surveillance report to construct an
uncertainty-aware anchor for associating high-rate anonymous
onboard measurements. The anchor reinforces task identity
under ambiguous associations and supports short-term track
continuity when reliable onboard measurement support is
unavailable. Rather than treating data association as an isolated
task, we consider a closed-loop cooperative tracking system.
The source code and configurations used in our evaluation are
available in an anonymized repository. 1
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
•We formulate a closed-loop cooperative key-target track-
ing problem that combines intermittent identity-bearing
surveillance reports with high-rate anonymous onboard
measurements in dense non-key-target interference.
•We propose a training-free IA-BP-JPDA method, which
employs a covariance-aware identity anchor to guide
BP-based measurement association and incorporates a
bounded fallback to handle temporary measurement loss.
•Experiments over paired Monte Carlo runs demon-
strate improved tracking accuracy, identity continuity, and
closed-loop pursuit over representative MTT baselines,
while ablation results verify the proposed association and
anchor-management mechanisms.

II. RELATEDWORK
A. Cooperative Multi-UAV Search and Tracking
Cooperative UA V search and tracking has been studied
from both learning-based and model-based perspectives. Xia et
al. [8] developed a MARL-based scheme for energy-efficient
cooperative target tracking using target-position information
from detection sensors, while the sensing process itself is
not explicitly modeled. In [9], multiple pursuer UA Vs learn
cooperative search-and-tracking policies under a predefined
linear constant-speed target-motion pattern. Such learning-
based approaches optimize cooperative mobility effectively but
depend on representative training dynamics, which may be
unavailable for unknown or adversarial UA V behavior. Zhang
et al. [10] jointly considered multi-UA V area coverage, in-
formation fusion, task assignment, and target-tracking control,
demonstrating how estimated target states can support model-
based cooperative mobility.
These studies mainly focus on UA V coordination and con-
trol once usable target information is available. Our work
instead addresses the preceding association problem: main-
taining task-designated key-target tracks from anonymous on-
board measurements amid numerous non-key targets and false
alarms. In the closed loop, association errors can propagate
through fusion and assignment to defender motion, thereby
degrading subsequent sensing geometry.
B. BP-Based Data Association and Prior-Assisted Multi-
Target Tracking
Data association is a central challenge in multi-target track-
ing under missed detections, false alarms, and competing
measurements. BP methods provide efficient approximations
to marginal target–measurement association probabilities and
are attractive for scalable MTT. Recent studies have improved
BP-based tracking from different perspectives: Kropfreiter
et al. [11] analyzed track coalescence and repulsion in BP,
JPDA, and MHT; Sun et al. [12] combined adaptive kernel
Kalman filtering with BP for maneuvering targets under clut-
ter; and [13] jointly estimated target states, nonuniform clut-
ter, and associations through robust message passing. These
approaches improve association inference, state estimation,
or clutter modeling from anonymous measurements, but do
not provide an external task-level identity source when key
and non-key targets exhibit similar kinematics. A locally
plausible association may therefore still correspond to the
wrong mission-designated target and recursively bias subse-
quent predictions.
Persistent labels provide another mechanism for maintaining
track continuity. Label-aware random-finite-set filters, includ-
ing the labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) filter [14] and theδ-
generalized labeled multi-Bernoulli (δ-GLMB) filter [15], at-
tach distinct labels to target states and support temporally con-
sistent trajectory estimation. Theδ-GLMB explicitly maintains
label-conditioned association-history hypotheses, whereas the
LMB provides a lower-complexity approximation. However,
persistent labels do not eliminate measurement-to-track ambi-
guity: when key and non-key targets have similar kinematics
and measurements are anonymous, incorrect associations can
still cause a labeled track to drift toward the wrong physical
target. This challenge is further aggravated in our setting by
intermittent and inaccurate surveillance reports and dense non-
key-target interference.
Complementary information has also been incorporated into
probabilistic association. Bai et al. [16] extracted range–
Doppler spectral features to estimate whether radar detections
were target- or clutter-generated and fused the resulting classi-
fication beliefs with kinematic beliefs obtained through factor-
graph inference, improving clutter suppression and association
robustness. More closely related to our work, Gaglione et
al. [17] fused anonymous sensor measurements with target-
provided reports carrying both state and identity information in
a unified factor graph. Their sum-product formulation accounts
for missed detections, false alarms, association uncertainty,
and asynchronous observations. Our problem similarly com-
bines identity-bearing and anonymous sensing information, but
considers a different multi-UA V key-target tracking setting.
The surveillance system provides intermittent and inaccu-
rate reports only for task-designated key targets, while each
defender UA V receives high-rate anonymous measurements
contaminated by numerous non-key targets and false alarms.
We therefore focus on maintaining reliable key-target identi-
ties under persistent measurement-association ambiguity using
identity-anchored BP-JPDA.

A. Closed-Loop Processing Overview
Unlike learning-based approaches that require offline pol-
icy training under predefined target-motion scenarios, IA-BP-
JPDA requires neither scenario-specific training trajectories
nor learned policy parameters. Instead, it operates recursively
using two online sensing streams: identity-bearing surveillance
reports and anonymous onboard measurements. As illustrated
in Fig. 1, IA-BP-JPDA operates in a closed sensing–tracking–
control loop. At each time stepn, the sensing layer provides
the anonymous onboard measurement sets{Z u,n}u∈U and the
identity-bearing surveillance reportsG q available at that time.
Each defender UA V processes both information streams using
IA-BP-JPDA to estimate its key-target tracks. The resulting
fused tracks are used for target assignment and UA V control,
after which the system advances ton+ 1with new sensing
and the loop continues.

A. Experiment Setup
We simulate 5 defender UA Vs, 3 key targets, and 30 non-key
targets in a200×200×20 m 3 ROI for30 swith∆t= 0.5 s.
All targets follow the 3D Gauss–Markov mobility model at
vT = 4 m/s. Non-key targets are initialized with headings
and pitches close to those of nearby key targets (with small
random perturbations of about0.02rad and0.01rad, respec-
tively), while their subsequent motion evolves independently.
The Gauss–Markov heading and pitch innovation standard
deviations are0.5and0.2rad, respectively.
The defender UA V speed is set tov U = 5 m/sand have
a70 monboard sensing range. The onboard position and
velocity measurement standard deviations are[2,2,0.4] mand
[1,1,0.2] m/s. Detection probability decreases from0.95at
short range to0.50near the sensing boundary and further
degrades with local target density. The false-alarm rate is
1 s −1, with half of the false alarms generated near visible
targets.

B. Baselines and Evaluation Metrics
We compare the proposed IA-BP-JPDA with four represen-
tative MTT baselines. All methods use the same sensor outputs
and motion models and differ only in how the identity-bearing
surveillance reports and anonymous onboard measurements
are processed. The four baselines are detailed as follows.
1) BP-based JPDA (BP-JPDA) [6]:The surveillance reports
initialize or reinitialize key-target tracks; once initialized,
the tracks are recursively predicted and updated using the
anonymous onboard measurements.
2) GM-PHD [21]:The surveillance reports generate birth
components and associate the extracted components with key-
target identities. The anonymous onboard measurements con-
stitute the measurement set for the recursive GM-PHD update.
3)δ-GLMB [15]:The surveillance reports provide labeled
track priors, while onboard measurements are used to recur-
sively update the joint hypotheses and target states.
4) LMB [14]:The LMB filter is a computationally efficient
approximation of theδ-GLMB that assumes independence
among Bernoulli components, using the same surveillance
reports and onboard measurements but with lower complexity.
Performance is evaluated using three complementary met-
rics covering set-level tracking accuracy, identity consistency,
and closed-loop target pursuit. All methods use identical
sensing realizations, fusion, assignment, and control, and
are evaluated over 100 paired Monte Carlo runs. We report

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper studied multi-UA V tracking of a small set of key
targets in the presence of numerous non-key targets. The task
is challenging because identity-bearing surveillance reports are
intermittent and inaccurate, while high-rate onboard measure-
ments are anonymous. We propose IA-BP-JPDA to address the
association ambiguity. It constructs uncertainty-aware identity
anchors via covariance-based fusion of recursive predictions
and surveillance reports, and then uses these anchors to
guide the BP-JPDA association of onboard measurements. A
bounded fallback maintains short-term track continuity when
onboard measurement support is temporarily unavailable.
IA-BP-JPDA is fully model-based and requires neither
labeled training data nor target-specific motion patterns or
historical trajectories. Experiments over 100 paired Monte
Carlo runs showed improved tracking accuracy, identity conti-
nuity, and closed-loop pursuit over representative MTT base-
lines. In particular, IA-BP-JPDA reduced the time-averaged
GOSPA by51.6%relative to BP-JPDA, achieved the low-
est IDSW among the compared methods, and operated well
within the0.5 ssampling interval. Sensitivity analysis showed
that the performance degrades gradually as distractor density
increases. The ablation results further confirmed the contri-
butions of the onboard measurement-association branch and
the uncertainty-aware anchor-management mechanism. Future
work will investigate adaptive uncertainty estimation, more
realistic communication-channel modeling, and the integration
of key-target tracking with subsequent cooperative multi-UA V
communication jamming.
